## 1. The five books as skillsets (what to actually do)

### 1.1 Business Model Generation — "describe, challenge, redesign"

**Skillset**
1. **Map one business model on one page** using the nine blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure.
2. **Read the dependencies:** right side = market; left side = what it takes to serve it; value proposition sits between.
3. **Treat the canvas as a claim, not a plan.** Date/version it; every block is a hypothesis until evidence arrives.
4. **Use patterns** (unbundling, long tail, multi-sided, freemium, open, platform) as design moves, not templates.
5. **Design with customers**, prototype several models, and tell the story of the model visually.
6. **Stress-test** each block: what breaks if this is wrong? Where is the margin? Who pays whom?

### 1.2 Value Proposition Design — "fit before you build"

**Skillset**
1. **Pick one customer segment** (not "everyone").
2. **Customer profile:** functional, social and emotional **jobs**; **pains** (undesired outcomes, obstacles, risks); **gains** (required, expected, desired, unexpected).
3. **Rank** jobs/pains/gains by importance and by how unsatisfied they are today.
4. **Value map:** products/services, **pain relievers**, **gain creators** — each one explicitly tied to a ranked item.
5. **Test fit:** only claim fit when customers confirm the matches with evidence; otherwise revise.
6. **Prototype the value proposition** (landing page, offer, pitch, service mock) before building product.
7. **Avoid the classic traps:** features without jobs, gains nobody asked for, averages that hide segments.

### 1.3 Testing Business Ideas — "evidence over opinion"

**Skillset**
1. **List assumptions** across **desirability, feasibility, viability**.
2. **Prioritize the riskiest** (high impact if wrong + high uncertainty).
3. **Turn assumptions into testable hypotheses** ("we believe X customers will do Y because Z").
4. **Pick the experiment** from the library (43–44 types) by cost, setup time, run time and **strength of evidence**.
5. **Discover first, validate later:** interviews, observation, fake door, concierge → then presales, pilots, A/B tests.
6. **Record a Test Card** (hypothesis, test, metric, success threshold) and a **Learning Card** (what we learned, decision).
7. **Decide: persevere / pivot / kill** — and make the next experiment the highest-risk remaining assumption.
8. **Make experimentation continuous**, not a one-off workshop.

### 1.4 The Invincible Company — "explore and exploit at the same time"

**Skillset**
1. **Separate two portfolios:** **exploit** (run and improve today's model) and **explore** (build tomorrow's).
2. **Map initiatives** on the Portfolio Map by innovation risk, expected return, and death/disruption risk.
3. **Manage the explore portfolio** through ideation → discovery → validation → growth; expect most ideas to die cheaply.
4. **Measure innovation differently:** hypothesis-level, business-model-level, portfolio-level metrics (not just P&L).
5. **Use business model patterns** (21 patterns) to compete beyond product features.
6. **Build a culture** that tolerates intelligent failure, rewards evidence, and runs ambidextrous leadership.
7. **Kill fast, scale deliberately:** Bosch weeds out ~90% of ideas that won't scale; the winners get real investment.
8. **Reinvent continuously** — the model that got you here may be the one that kills you.

### 1.5 High-Impact Tools for Teams — "alignment is the multiplier"

**Skillset**
1. **Team Alignment Map (TAM)** in four pillars: **joint objectives, joint commitments, joint resources, joint risks.**
2. **Planning mode** at kickoff; **assessment mode** as a rapid health check before things break.
3. **Forward pass** (objectives → resources/risks) and **backward pass** (deadline → dependencies/feasibility).
4. **Team Contract:** values in action, communication agreements, conflict resolution, respect indicators.
5. **Fact Finder:** separate facts from interpretations; ask clarifying questions; validate understanding.
6. **Nonviolent Requests:** observation → feelings → needs → specific request.
7. **Respect Card:** recognize specific behaviors, regularly.
8. **Review rhythm:** reveal → reflect → repair; keep the artifacts alive in meetings.

## 2. Applying the method to Settled

### 2.1 Choose the segment (VPD step 1)

**Primary segment:** **enterprise merchants with high-AOV, high-trust-deficit commerce** (high-ticket DTC, luxury resale, marketplaces, independent brands) — buyer: VP Payments / Head of Risk / CFO.
**Secondary:** **platforms/PSPs enabling agentic checkout** (marketplace operators, agent platforms) — buyer: Head of Payments / Head of AI Commerce.
**Tertiary:** **agent operators** (procurement agents, shopping agents) — buyer: Head of Agent Product.
Do not pitch "everyone who sells online."

### 2.2 Customer profile — primary segment

**Jobs**
- Functional: convert first-time buyers; keep fraud/chargeback losses low; resolve disputes fast; prepare for agent-initiated orders; grow GMV without discounting.
- Social: be seen as a safe, modern merchant; not be the brand that got scammed by an AI agent.
- Emotional: stop worrying that a new buyer or a bot is a loss.

**Pains (ranked)**
1. Trust doubt kills conversion: 51% cite business legitimacy as a reason to abandon; 96% are concerned about unfamiliar stores.
2. Protection is a bundled, opaque cost centre — no risk-based price, no portable proof.
3. Chargebacks/disputes are slow and expensive; ops teams drown in manual work.
4. Badges are paid theater; platform scores don't travel.
5. Agent traffic is arriving without a trust/recourse layer; accepting it feels like opening a fraud hole.
6. Returns/claims are adversarial and slow; buyers churn.

**Gains (ranked)**
1. Higher conversion from first-time/unfamiliar buyers.
2. Lower loss rate and claims cost per order.
3. Safe, measurable agent-driven GMV.
4. Instant resolution → better reviews, NPS, repeat.
5. A portable credential that lifts every channel.
6. Risk-based pricing that rewards good behavior.

### 2.3 Value map — and fit

| Pain/gain | Settled pain reliever / gain creator | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Trust doubt kills conversion | Signed **Merchant Trust Credential** (outcome-derived, portable, 94% coverage, confidence-quantified) | A/B conversion lift vs control |
| Protection is opaque/bundled | **Risk-priced protection**: tier → 35/90/150 bps → cap → capture/escrow, reserve-backed | Pilot loss ratio + merchant WTP |
| Chargebacks slow/expensive | **Buyer/agent-initiated instant claim** paid from merchant reserve in ~1.2s via PayPal refund | Time-to-refund, claim cost vs chargeback |
| Badges/platform scores don't travel | **Portable VC** accepted across stores/PSPs/agents | Cross-party acceptance test |
| Agent traffic is uninsured | **Agent Passport** (operator KYB, mandate, spend limits, reputation) + **Agent Commerce Contract** | Agent sandbox with one platform |
| Returns/claims adversarial | Rule-based adjudication + appeal + recovery | Dispute resolution cycle time |
| Higher conversion | Trust credential + instant guarantee at checkout | Holdout experiment |
| Safe agent GMV | Passport + priced protection + contract | Agent pilot GMV/loss |

**Fit statement (to test, not assert):** *the highest-importance, highest-unsatisfied jobs are "convert unfamiliar buyers" and "accept agent purchases without new loss."* The value map addresses both, but **fit is unproven** until the experiments in §2.6 pass.

### 2.4 The value proposition (the one to test)

**For enterprise merchants who lose first-time buyers to trust doubts and are about to accept AI-agent orders:**

> **Settled turns every order into a signed, insured deal. A portable, outcome-derived trust credential proves the merchant is real; risk-priced protection caps the downside; and if anything fails, the buyer is refunded in seconds from the merchant's reserve — so you grow revenue from strangers and agents without carrying the risk.**

**Short version (10 words):** *Sell to strangers and agents — protected, priced, and paid back in seconds.*

**For platforms/PSPs:**
> *Give your agents a trust API: verify the merchant, price the protection, settle the contract, and pay the buyer back instantly.*

**For agent operators:**
> *A passport that lets your agent spend safely — with a mandate, a trusted merchant, and a remedy that doesn't need a human.*

### 2.5 Why this is a *great* value proposition — and what it replaces

| Test | Settled | Trust badge (TrustedSite/BuySafe) | Chargeback guarantee (Signifyd) | Buyer protection (PayPal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Important job | convert + protect + agent-ready | badge only | merchant fraud only | post-hoc disputes |
| Unique | portable outcome credential + priced protection + instant payout | no outcomes, no portability | merchant-facing indemnity | free, bundled, platform-bound |
| Credible | working prototype, PayPal SDK, reserve ledger, instant refund | certification | established but not portable | trusted but slow |
| Measurable | conversion lift, loss ratio, time-to-refund, agent GMV | conversion only | chargeback rate | dispute rate |
| Defensible | payment data + reserve + network + agent API | badge brand | underwriting model | distribution only |

**The "truly great" claim:** it is the only proposition that makes **trust portable, protection priced, and recourse instant** at the same time — the three things a merchant needs to sell to an unknown buyer or an agent.

### 2.6 Riskiest assumptions + experiment plan (TBI)

| # | Assumption | Risk | Experiment | Evidence | Success threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Merchants will pay bps for protection on top of processing | Viability | 10 pricing interviews + LOI offer at 10/25/50 bps | Medium | 3/10 sign LOI at ≥25 bps |
| A2 | Trust credential lifts first-time-buyer conversion | Desirability | Holdout A/B on one merchant (credential+guarantee vs control) | High | ≥5% relative lift, p<0.1 |
| A3 | Instant reserve claims beat chargebacks on cost/time | Feasibility/impact | Concierge pilot: 20 claims adjudicated manually | High | time-to-refund <1 day; cost/claim < chargeback cost |
| A4 | Buyers/merchants accept a third-party credential | Desirability | Landing page + 20 buyer interviews + merchant pilot | Medium | 40% of interviewees would trust it; 1 pilot signs |
| A5 | Platforms will integrate a trust API | Distribution | 5 platform/payments-lead interviews + sandbox LOI | Medium | 1 sandbox pilot |
| A6 | No-custody reserve is compliant and insurable | Feasibility | Insurer/PSP partner interviews + counsel memo | Medium | 1 insurer term sheet / counsel sign-off |
| A7 | Agent operators need a passport + contract | Desirability | Agent sandbox with 1 operator; 10 agent-platform interviews | Medium | 1 operator integrates; 3 want it |

**Order:** discovery interviews first (cheap, fast), then fake-door/landing page, then concierge pilot, then A/B and pricing. Record Test Cards and Learning Cards; decide persevere/pivot/kill after each wave.

### 2.8 Portfolio Map (Invincible Company)

- **Exploit:** trust credential + priced protection for existing merchants — near-term revenue, 70% of effort.
- **Explore:** agent passport + ACC + agent trust API — the growth engine, 20%.
- **Explore (longer):** B2B credit/insurance and risk-data products — 10%.
- **Metrics:** hypothesis-level (assumptions tested), model-level (protected GMV, loss ratio), portfolio-level (explore/exploit balance, time-to-evidence).
- **Kill rule:** if A2 (conversion lift) and A1 (WTP) fail in 30 days, pivot to the platform/agent API wedge; don't scale a badge.

### 2.9 Team Alignment Map (hackathon team)

- **Joint objectives:** a live 5-step demo, all five axes visibly 8+, a 4-minute story.
- **Joint commitments:** Lead = integration/demo script; backend = APIs + tests; frontend = demo console; QA = red tests + fallback screenshots.
- **Joint resources:** PayPal sandbox, local server, screenshots, recorded backup, reset script.
- **Joint risks:** live API failure, judge questions on novelty, time overrun, Wi-Fi. Mitigations: SIM mode default, deterministic reset, backup video, Q&A cheat sheet.
- **Team contract:** one owner per file; demo decisions by Lead; "no new features after T-30 min"; evidence over opinion.

## 3. The 30-day validation plan after the hackathon

| Week | Do | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 merchant interviews + 5 platform/agent interviews; collect dispute/chargeback data | Are the jobs and pains real? |
| 2 | Landing page + pricing conversations; 10 LOI asks at 25 bps | Will they pay? |
| 3 | Concierge pilot on one merchant (20 orders/claims); holdout design | Does it work and lift conversion? |
| 4 | Agent sandbox with one operator; insurer/PSP term sheet; counsel memo | Is the model feasible and insurable? |

**Persevere** if A1 + A2 pass; **pivot** to platform/agent API if only A5/A7 pass; **kill** the credential if neither conversion nor WTP moves.
